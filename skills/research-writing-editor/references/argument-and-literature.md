# Argument and literature

Use for motivation, related-work synthesis, contribution identity, and the connection between evidence and the proposed approach.

## Find the problem before naming the solution

Reconstruct the intended argument in ordinary language: the research setting, what is known, the observation or difficulty that remains, its consequence, the angle taken here, and the resulting contribution. This is a diagnostic aid, not a compulsory six-sentence paragraph.

A problem can arise from an empirical discrepancy, a mechanism that competing accounts explain differently, a mathematical question, a limitation under specified conditions, an interpretive ambiguity in sources, or a consequential design constraint. It need not be a missing algorithm or a low benchmark score.

Ask whether the problem would still be meaningful if the proposed technique were unavailable. If it disappears without the technique's name, the motivation may have been reverse-engineered.

| Statement | Editorial diagnosis | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing work lacks our integrated module. | Names the solution's absence. | Establish the affected phenomenon or operation and the evidence for its consequence. |
| Deployment still requires matching records. | Lists a task. | Explain the observed ambiguity or failure in matching, if supported, and why it matters. |
| Prior methods fail in realistic settings. | Unspecified scope and evidence. | Identify the tested conditions and actual failure, or remove the generalization. |
| These sources disagree about the event's sequence. | Potential interpretive question. | Explain what differs, how the sources support each reading, and why resolving the sequence changes the account. |

Do not invent a deficiency merely to bridge two paragraphs. Conversely, do not delete the entire problem statement because one formulation was unsupported. Locate the supported question and express it.

## Give evidence its appropriate reach

Use source passages or supplied results to support a claim about prior work. Distinguish a direct report, the present authors' inference, and a research proposal. A single paper can document its own limitations; it rarely establishes what an entire field does or lacks.

If source contents are unavailable, do not fabricate mechanism details from a title or abstract. Work with the available evidence, mark the exact point requiring verification outside the manuscript, and use the environment's retrieval tools if authorized and necessary. Do not stop all unrelated editing for one missing citation.

Broad assertions such as 'research mainly focuses on', 'resources remain scarce', and 'no method can' need a defined scope and appropriate coverage. Replace them with a supported, concrete account, not an automatic retreat such as 'we do not claim a comprehensive survey'.

## Synthesize without flattening

Organize literature by the question it answers, shared mechanism, assumption, or evidence type. Explain what each route contributes to understanding, how routes relate, and which boundary matters for the present question.

Preserve enough of the mechanism for a non-specialist to understand the comparison. 'Uses a factor graph' does not explain which objects are connected or what the factors express. A concise operational explanation may be longer and more useful.

Name the researcher when a specific method or finding needs attribution. 'Related methods' is acceptable only when the antecedent genuinely denotes several methods with that property. Avoid alternating between a named study and an ambiguous plural summary.

A paragraph need not contain all steps below, but the reader should be able to follow the relationship:

- what problem the cited work addresses;
- how it treats that problem;
- what common understanding emerges;
- why a particular unresolved issue or new angle follows.

Do not append 'however, it does not consider our task' to each citation. A scope difference is often a neutral distinction, not a flaw. Explain the relevant boundary once at the level where it matters.

## Compare contributions at the mechanism level

For close prior work, compare the aspects that determine the result: research object, representation, unit of analysis or decision, fixed and variable quantities, decision process, constraints, evidence scale, and intended use. Select the relevant dimensions rather than dumping a universal comparison table into the paper.

Shared vocabulary does not establish equivalence. A construction hierarchy, dependency hierarchy, and administrative hierarchy may have different meanings. Equally, a renamed technique is not a new mechanism.

State the contribution positively and specifically. A new decision unit, analysis that distinguishes competing explanations, or documented source-to-model interpretation can be a meaningful advance without claiming the first use of every component. Avoid ranking originality as 'breakthrough' or predicting acceptance without supporting evidence.

## Maintain the Introduction's trajectory

Establish the setting and object, develop the knowledge relevant to the question, explain the concrete issue, then introduce the approach and contribution. Adapt this trajectory to the field. Do not create an encyclopedia or force every domain into a technology-deficit narrative.

Keep implementation previews concentrated where they answer the established question. If every related-work paragraph ends with 'we propose', the method may be arriving repeatedly before the motivation is ready. If the discussion jumps from a list of papers to the method, restore the missing problem analysis. Replacing an unsupported criticism with “we examine joint choices” can still leave that jump intact. Explain the supported relationship that makes the choices interdependent. For example, when objects occupy the same limited area, placing one changes the area available to the others. This explains the decision problem without asserting that all prior methods fail. If even that relationship is not supplied or defensible from the stated setup, identify the missing evidence outside the manuscript rather than manufacturing a gap.

Before accepting a revision, ask what question a reader now wants answered. If the only answer is 'what did the authors implement?', the motivation may still be underdeveloped.
