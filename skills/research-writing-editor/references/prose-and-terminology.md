# Prose and terminology

Use for sentence stiffness, abrupt connections, abstract nouns, audit or defensive voice, and excessive compression. Judge each expression by its function in context, not a blacklist.

## Diagnose the reading obstacle

A technically correct sentence can be difficult because its subject is hidden, its modifier has several possible targets, its pronoun lacks an antecedent, or its relation to the previous sentence is unstated. Identify that obstacle before replacing words.

A useful internal paraphrase is: who or what acts, on which object, under which condition, with what result? If this cannot be answered from the passage and supplied evidence, resolve the ambiguity before polishing. Do not guess an algorithm, estimator, or source interpretation.

Distinguish four editorial choices: retain; repair a concrete reading obstacle; correct a factual or logical error; verify a consequential fact. These choices guide work; they need not become a table delivered to the author on every task.

## Keep explanatory content

A short sentence can be dense and a long sentence can be clear. Preserve definitions, concrete operations, and comparisons that let the reader understand the argument. Do not replace an intelligible explanation with a string of labels such as 'hierarchical topology-aware configuration optimization'.

Combine sentences when one supplies the condition or explanation for the other. Split when several independent claims compete for the main clause. Vary rhythm naturally. Do not enforce a maximum word count, ban passive voice, or attach a takeaway to every paragraph.

Use connective words only for an actual relationship. 'Therefore' needs an inference, 'further' a meaningful development, 'similarly' a relevant shared property. When the connection is missing, name the shared object or explain the link instead of adding a transition adverb.

## Recognize voices that obstruct research prose

| Voice | What it does | Typical repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defensive | Anticipates an unmade objection and repeatedly denies possible overreadings | State the finding and any condition that actually changes its meaning |
| Audit | Reports internal verification, frozen states, approvals, or completeness as if these were scientific findings | Keep relevant design facts once; remove project-status language |
| Developer notebook | Enumerates helpers, folders, exceptions, and debugging history without a research sequence | Explain objects and dependencies; place essential implementation detail appropriately |
| Self-grading | Calls the study weak, robust, rigorous, or important instead of showing why | Report evidence and explain its implication |
| Automatic significance | Ends each paragraph with generic value or future promises | Give a concrete consequence if one follows; otherwise stop |

A Methods sentence about an inclusion rule or an actual validation experiment can be necessary. Its formal tone alone is not audit voice. Likewise, a negative result or precise scope statement can be informative. Apply the evidence guide before deleting a qualifier.

Prefer statements of what the object is and what the result shows. A contrast is valuable when the contrast itself advances the argument; do not mechanically remove all 'but' or 'rather than'.

## Stabilize concepts, not just strings

Maintain the mapping between referents and names. Distinguish a model from an instance, a measurement from an index, an index from its penalty in an objective, a candidate from a selected combination, and raw evidence from an interpretation or depiction.

For a genuinely central concept, introduce the name and definition when needed. Later uses can employ an unambiguous short form. Define abbreviations within the appropriate reading unit; an abstract and main text may each need a definition.

Use established domain terms accurately. Do not substitute vague everyday phrases for a defined technical quantity. When a familiar-looking noun is project-specific, explain its referent. 'Configuration' may be accurate in a formal definition and vague in a sentence about actual physical changes.

Do not create new compound labels to make ordinary operations seem novel. Conversely, do not erase a useful coined concept merely because it is new; assess whether it names a distinct, repeatedly used object.

## Chinese prose

Chinese can omit the subject when the actor is obvious. Restore it when the operation, evidence ownership, or causal relation would otherwise be ambiguous. Prefer direct verbs over repeated '进行…的实现' constructions.

Repeated full labels such as '相对…控制模式' may be shortened after definition if the meaning stays clear. Avoid unnatural compressed nouns such as joining two object names into an unexplained measurement. Spell out the relationship when necessary.

Do not treat '仍需', '配置', '记录', '约束', or '然而' as forbidden words. Ask whether the sentence names a real object or issue, advances the argument, and helps the reader.

## Punctuation and tone

Use punctuation to express syntax, not as a signature style. Repeated semicolons, em-dash asides, colons, or parenthetical caveats can fragment prose, but each can be appropriate. Do not impose a universal punctuation quota or change mathematical minus signs, ranges, identifiers, or established notation during prose cleanup.

Preserve the manuscript's mature voice. Neither uniformly conversational wording nor inflated academic diction is inherently clearer. The final read should feel like an account of research, not a conversation with the editor or a list of compliance assurances.
