# Bilingual revision

Use for translation, parallel-language manuscripts, and synchronization after edits. Preserve scientific meaning while allowing each language its own syntax.

## Establish the relationship between versions

Identify the authoritative version and the requested direction of change. Do not assume the English draft is always a translation of the latest Chinese file. Check which passages have been revised and whether the user wants direct updates, a suggested translation, or a comparison.

Separate shared semantic content from language-specific organization. Match the research object, conditions, quantities, sequence, comparison direction, and claim strength. Sentences need not align one to one.

## Translate the action and relation

Find the subject and main verb early in English. A Chinese sequence of '针对…在…条件下，通过…实现…' often needs reordering, not a literal string of introductory clauses.

Prefer verbs that describe the actual operation. Selecting a candidate, adjusting a physical parameter, fitting a curve, and grouping regions are not interchangeable. 'Recover' may imply a pre-existing object; use it only when that meaning fits the method.

A passive sentence is appropriate when the procedure or object is the focus. Do not convert clear passive prose into wordy active nominalizations. Do not insert 'we' in every sentence to create false fluency.

## Keep referents precise

Examples of decisions, not mandatory replacements:

- A count of annotated examples is not necessarily a count of annotation labels.
- An illustration displays the item that received a rating; the figure number itself was not evaluated.
- A term for a whole-group operation may wrongly exclude supported component-level operations.
- 'Higher objective values' identifies the quantity being compared more explicitly than a generic performance verb.
- A named ordinal level usually needs a grammatical construction such as 'at the high level'; a bare 'in high' may not be idiomatic.

Use the established technical term for the relevant domain. A broad English word may not preserve a Chinese distinction, and two similar Chinese words may translate to different technical concepts. Check the original source when a specialized translation matters.

## Time, number, and logical force

Use tense consistently with the account: general mechanisms, completed procedures, and observed findings have different roles. A paper can use several tenses; avoid switching without a change of role within one completed case.

Preserve all/each/some, per-group/per-sample, within/between, and fixed/variable distinctions. Translation must not turn a condition into a guarantee, a suggestion into a demonstrated result, or a comparison into a causal explanation.

Keep symbols, units, labels, and citation referents aligned. A term may need a short explanation in one language rather than a new acronym in both.

## Abstract limits and revision

A constrained abstract may require information redistribution. Decide which background detail can be compressed while preserving the problem, mechanism, and major result. Do not simply add precision until the word limit is exceeded or silently delete essential conditions to meet it.

After translation, read it as a standalone passage, then compare meaning against the source. Check transitions in the target language rather than relying only on a sentence-by-sentence checklist. If no scientific content changed, do not imply the whole paper has been independently reverified.
