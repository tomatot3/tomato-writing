# Section writing

Use to determine what information belongs at a given point in the manuscript. These are functional distinctions, not required headings, paragraph counts, or a universal IMRaD structure.

## Abstract

Make the object, problem, approach, central result, and significance intelligible together. Use a concrete opening suited to the paper rather than a generic claim that a task 'requires' the authors' chosen capabilities.

Define an unfamiliar central unit briefly. Keep material acquisition, processing, and inference in their actual order. If the study collected images and then annotated them, do not imply that pre-existing annotations were collected.

Select results that answer the question. Counts of sources, trials, comparisons, assets, and users have different meanings; a chain of unexplained numbers is not an evidence summary. Retain the conditions needed to understand the main result without reciting every control.

Describe the actual output: a structural representation, prediction, physical prototype, interpretation, or deployed intervention. Do not imply a complete product or automated workflow when only a component or manually assisted demonstration was evaluated.

When a venue imposes a limit, verify the applicable limit and count the current abstract. Reallocate information before stripping essential semantics. Parallel-language abstracts need matching scientific meaning, not identical sentence order.

## Introduction

Use the argument guide. Its job is to establish why the question is worth answering and how the contribution follows from existing knowledge. Specific methods need enough explanation to support comparison; the Introduction need not reproduce their implementation.

A paper's contribution can be conceptual, empirical, methodological, interpretive, or resource-based. Describe the new knowledge or capability, not merely completed activities.

## Methods or analytical approach

Begin with the objects and dependencies a reader needs to understand the work. Establish the input and output where relevant. Explain major stages before enumerating local settings. Distinguish:

- representation: what the objects and relationships mean;
- construction or measurement: how observations or candidates are obtained;
- feasibility or inclusion: which items qualify;
- evaluation: how quantities or preferences are measured;
- selection or inference: how conclusions or outputs are obtained.

These distinctions can occur in continuous prose; they are not mandatory subsections. Qualitative source analysis should explain selection, interpretation, comparison, and the basis for judgments rather than imitate an optimization pipeline.

Describe an equation's purpose and newly introduced symbols at the point needed. A familiar formula does not justify leaving project-specific variables undefined. Explain a function briefly if its role is unclear, but avoid turning the section into a general tutorial.

Separate general mechanism from prototype-specific constants, apparatus details, and exceptional code paths. Retain details that affect reproducibility or a claim's validity, in the location permitted by the venue and helpful to the reader. Do not automatically move all complex material to SI.

Notebook-like prose often mixes rationale, parameter values, implementation status, and exceptions in one sequence. Reorganize by scientific dependency. Do not simply delete the hard parts or attach a purpose sentence to every operation.

## Results or findings

Let a paragraph answer a research question or establish a finding. Identify the comparison and direction, present the evidence needed to assess it, and explain its immediate meaning. An opening number can be effective when its meaning is already clear; not every paragraph needs 'To evaluate...'.

Distinguish observations from explanations. Keep procedural detail only where needed to understand the result. Use figures and tables for full distributions or parallel values, and text for the important pattern; avoid repeating the entire table.

A worked example can explain a mechanism without representing its frequency. Make the example's role clear and use aggregate evidence for aggregate claims. Report useful null and mixed results, especially when they distinguish explanations or affect interpretation.

## Discussion

Explain what the findings add to the question, how they relate to existing knowledge, and why the mechanism or interpretation matters. Avoid repeating figure-by-figure observations or the complete application workflow.

Keep attribution clear: prior authors' findings, this study's results, and the current interpretation should be distinguishable. A cluster of citations at the paragraph end should not obscure ownership of claims.

Discuss limitations when they change interpretation, use, or a concrete next question. State the relevant scope directly. Do not append hypothetical failure cases after every positive finding, apologize for ordinary design choices, or grade the study's own quality.

Future directions should identify a specific unresolved mechanism, boundary condition, source ambiguity, or operational obstacle. Explain what could be investigated and what that would clarify, without promising work the authors have not undertaken.

## Application case and resources

Connect the case to the paper's central question. Assign different functions to its appearances: motivation in the Introduction, essential operations in Methods, demonstrated changes in Results, and implications in Discussion. Do not repeat the whole workflow at every mention.

State manual intervention and actual editing or use capabilities accurately. A visual mockup demonstrates a design possibility, not automatically usability, efficiency, preservation benefit, or external validity.

Explain what a resource contains and allows others to do. Raw sources, annotations, generated outputs, and evaluated samples can be related without being the same dataset. Update membership claims only when the actual resource supports them.

## Title, headings, and supporting material

A title should identify the actual research task or contribution without confusing an intermediate output with the final application. Technical names are optional unless their recognition helps the reader; changing the title should not silently change the task's scope.

Headings should help readers find conceptual stages. Avoid turning every operation into a miniature heading. Numbering, Methods location, supplementary policy, abstract limits, and citation style are venue-specific and must be verified when they matter.

The main text should support basic understanding without requiring a reader to reconstruct the argument from SI. SI can extend evidence and detail while keeping accurate links to the final main text.

## Cross-section repetition and cumulative reading

A passage can be clear in isolation and still make the paper tiring. Repeating a mechanism or qualification in new words can repeatedly reset the reader to the beginning. Review information by meaning and function, not just matching words or counting occurrences.

For a whole-paper or linked-section request, follow recurring explanations through the reading order. Ask what is already established and what this occurrence adds. Choose a primary location for the full explanation. Elsewhere, keep the part needed for a new purpose, use an unambiguous short reference, or remove the repeated explanation. Do not append a reminder simply because a hypothetical reader might have forgotten.

Useful recurrence changes the reader's understanding: an abstract introduces the central idea; Methods defines it and gives its procedure; Results reports what happened under that procedure; Discussion explains what the finding means. Repeating the procedure before interpreting every result does not perform a new function. Likewise, a framework overview, contribution list and Methods opening should not all retell the same sequence at the same level of detail. Assign them distinct jobs rather than automatically deleting any one of them.

Conditions need the same judgment. Define a shared experimental condition in the design section. Retain a compact condition beside a comparison if omitting it would change the apparent claim. Remove later enumerations that add no new scope information. In Discussion, carry a condition forward when its consequence matters, rather than merely reminding readers that it exists. A boundary discussed once does not require a disclaimer after every positive result.

Do not enforce “say it only once.” Abstracts, figure legends, table notes and SI may need to stand alone. This permits necessary recurrence; it is not a reason to add every design detail to an already adequate abstract or caption. Likewise, do not retain a reminder solely to prevent an unsupported hypothetical overreading. Formal definitions may expand brief earlier introductions. Repeated terminology helps readers track the same object; replacing it with synonyms can make the paper harder to follow. Keep numerical uncertainty, manual intervention, or other facts where they determine the meaning of a reported result. A source-analysis explanation and an algorithmic definition may concern the same object while adding different knowledge.

In an analysis, show the actual recurring passages, their locations and functions, then specify which occurrence to retain, shorten or remove. In a revision, reread across the affected sections in order: check that the first full explanation still exists, later references remain intelligible, and Results and Discussion advance beyond Methods. Do not create a separate audit workflow for a one-sentence edit.
