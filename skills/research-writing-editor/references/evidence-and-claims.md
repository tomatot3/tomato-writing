# Evidence and claim strength

Use whenever wording could change what was observed, calculated, inferred, or demonstrated. This guide preserves evidential meaning; it does not replace statistical analysis, theorem verification, ethics review, or source verification.

## Match the claim to its support

| Evidence | Usually supports | Additional support needed for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An observed association | Association under the stated design | A causal effect |
| Higher value of a defined objective | Improvement on that objective | Better perceived quality, utility, or real-world outcomes |
| A completed interactive demonstration | The operations actually demonstrated | Greater efficiency or general usability |
| Source comparisons and documented interpretations | An argued representation or reading | Exhaustive historical classification or unique reconstruction |
| A proof with explicit assumptions | The result under those assumptions | Unrestricted optimality or convergence |
| A released collection | Its actual contents and reuse possibilities | Widespread adoption or field-level resource scarcity |

These are distinctions, not instructions to weaken all prose. Strong evidence deserves a direct statement. Do not replace a demonstrated result with 'may potentially' to sound cautious.

## Keep meaningful conditions; remove imagined debate

A condition is load-bearing if removing it changes the proposition, estimator, procedure, or outcome. Examples include random assignment, measured covariates, the population, a search stopping rule, a finite candidate set, blinding, missing-data treatment, or manual correction in a claimed workflow.

Preserve such a condition in an economical location. Repeated reminders at every mention are usually unnecessary. Statements about interpretations no reasonable reader would infer can usually be removed.

For a capped exact search, distinguish complete termination from budget interruption. Do not infer global optimality if the search stopped early. If no feasible complete solution exists or was found, do not invent one merely to simplify the return description. The necessary level of detail depends on the claim and the available algorithm definition.

## Select numerical evidence by purpose

Identify the quantity being estimated or summarized, its unit, the comparison, and the level of aggregation. A mean, a median of paired differences, and a difference of medians are distinct. A range across experimental settings is not a confidence interval.

Confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, and precise estimates are ordinary scientific tools. Neither add them reflexively nor remove them as an 'AI style' marker. Keep uncertainty information that is required or informative for the inference. If an analysis has not been performed, do not invent a test, interval, or p-value while editing prose.

Reduce numerical clutter by choosing the main finding for text and preserving supporting detail in an appropriate table or supplement. Do not conceal relevant unfavourable results, replace actual values, or use selective reporting to make the study harder to challenge.

A zero median and a positive mean can coexist in a skewed distribution. Explain the distribution if it matters. A high score concentrated at the upper end may show acceptable performance under the scale but have little resolution for comparing methods; do not infer comparative superiority without the comparison.

## Preserve experimental conditions

Actual differences in timing, hardware, parallelism, data selection, or preprocessing cannot be relabeled as identical conditions for smoother prose. Decide whether the difference belongs in the main narrative, a settings table, or supporting material based on its effect on the claim.

Changing seeds or analysis settings requires the corresponding results to be updated. Do not choose a version because it looks stronger or describe a recommended candidate as author-approved. Selection rules and exclusions must remain faithful to the research record.

## Separate representations and outputs

A model, measured instance, rendering, annotation, vector object, and visualization can refer to different levels of a workflow. State which object is produced or edited. Re-selecting a stored candidate is not the same as modifying its geometry; grouping regions is not automatically recovering pre-existing semantic objects.

For source-driven research, distinguish visible evidence, researcher interpretation, and formalization. Traceability supports inspection and comparison, but does not mean every user must revisit raw sources for every operation.

## Respond proportionately to review

Evaluate a suggestion against the actual text, claim, and evidence. A request for wording precision may need one sentence; a new causal or comparative claim may need new research. Do not convert every potential objection into an experiment or defensive paragraph.

Avoid unsupported ratings of acceptance probability or the work's standing in a field. Report what the evidence permits and what precise change would resolve the issue. Keep the review discussion outside the manuscript unless it contributes a useful scientific statement.
